# Vignette 090 – To the Truth

> The sky was stained scarlet tonight, and at its zenith was Solana's Nexus Point.
>
> Solana had just tumbled through this point, landing among salt-scrubbed dunes along a horizon-split sun. Instead of the pajamas she went to bed in, she now bore silken robes, grains of sand clinging to them.
>
> As she removes herself from the sand angel in her wake, a rift opens in front of her. Through the rift was another place—a domain characterized by stern, metal walls, the faint hum of electronics, and an ever-present melancholic air.
>
> Perhaps, to some, Alis's Headscape wasn't the most inviting of sights, but to her, it was almost home-like.
>
> A cool breeze slinks through the rift. Solana thought it was quite pleasant—inviting, even. Taking this as a cue, she bounds in.

---

> "Good evening, Solana."
>
> That was Alis's voice. It boomed from nowhere in particular in this Headscape, its tone needling the line between firm and relaxed.
>
> Solana swings her arms metronomically to the blue sky above. "Hey!" she cries, a smile in her voice. "Is there anything I should know before I head in?"
>
> "The objective is unchanged," Alis mildly responds. "There is a truth at the center of this domain you will unveil. However, the terms by which you will reach it have altered."
>
> _Unveil the truth,_ Solana thinks. The last time she was here, she had learned brief fragments of this "truth". Tonight, she was bound to uncover the rest. She replies with a nod to aether.
>
> Alis's voice surfaces again. "I take it you understand our conditions, then," he says, a soupçon of fancy in his delivery. "In this case, I bid you good luck."
>
> Solana felt tingly. It was going to be one of those days.

---

> Alis's Headscape changed since last time.
>
> The first change was with the layout. The perimeter of the place was originally a large, intricate maze lined with dark, steely walls. Today, the corridors seemed much more straightforward, and while not day-bright by any means, the walls were noticeably clean and white.
>
> The second change was the atmosphere. Last time, the traps and locks in his Headscape were designed as something of a security system to keep trespassers out. This time, the configuration presented itself more as a challenge, a gauntlet to be completed to reveal the secrets that lay within.
>
> These changes made sense. After all, Alis would have no reason to discourage Solana from entering—she was the one, after all, who asked him about uncovering the truth.

---

> ...Though, even if this configuration was designed as a gauntlet, Alis didn't make it any easier for her. It was quite the opposite, actually.
>
> The last time she was here, she had learned 1. to avoid the cracks between the floor tiles, and 2. to decode the puzzle at the maze's end.
>
> This time, she also had to remember 1. the angle at which metal beams in the walls thrust out from to avoid being crushed, 2. the weak points in the hard light constructs to strike before they diced her, 3. SPEARMINT wasn't the only password to the gates, and 4. the sound of relentless ticking meant she couldn't dawdle before the walls closed in on her, and 5. through all this, Alis had adapted the gauntlet to her newfound shapeshifting abilities.
>
> By the fortieth or so "death", she was starting to get the hang of things.

---

> It was thirty or so additional "deaths" later that Solana, hair frayed, clothes caked, lungs grasping, and legs rickety, unlocked the final gate—the one that led from the Headscape's outer perimeter to its inner chamber.
>
> She figured the inner chamber evolved alongside the rest of the Headscape, and even before the gate fully opened, the air from within tickled her sinuses.
>
> The next thing she noticed was how pleasant the grass that hit her face was. Her legs, just now, didn't carry her a step farther, and in lieu of them, the grass cushioned her fall.
>
> She would be fine, Solana tells herself—she would just use this as a moment to rest her eyes.

---

> When Solana comes to, she was somewhere familiar yet unfamiliar.
>
> She had woken to the inside of a somewhat small room. The walls were made of metal, and the only light came from stray diodes along these walls. Complex-looking electronics organized themselves around the available space. Against the half-light, it looked like the roots of a magnolia tree were crawling through the ceiling panels and inward. The air smelled stale, as if it hadn't circulated in years. The verdure of a park that once seemed imminent now seemed far off.
>
> Solana suddenly realizes she wasn't lying on the ground. Instead, she had awoken on a bed made of metal, curved, suspended in midair. Furthermore, the metal shape was lined by Alis's coat—comfortable enough that she didn't notice at first. She could only assume that he had been courteous and brought her here.
>
> She tells her legs to rock back and forth, and they obey her. She tells her body to tap the floor, and it does.

---

> As soon as she sets one foot down, the room thrums. The circuitry lining the walls turns a luminous orange hue, and one of the displays flashes on.
>
> An array of light shapes projects before Solana, taking the form of three "inputs" and a scattering of shards. Each shard had a different shape, as though they were fragments from a pane of broken glass. More interestingly, each shard had what appeared to be a faint symbol inscribed onto it.
>
> This was a decoding puzzle. The three inputs were meant for the passwords she solved for outside of the chamber. Once the inputs were submitted, the glyphs would become converted to their original meaning. From here, she could arrange the glyphs in the right order.
>
> She knew that, now, there would be no forestalling the truth.
