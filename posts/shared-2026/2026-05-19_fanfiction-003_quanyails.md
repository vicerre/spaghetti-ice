# Fanfiction 003

> My dream took the form of an anime movie.
>
> The movie starts off with the context for the world we were about to see. In short, someone had slighted Alis, and in retaliation, Alis decided to refashion the world.
>
> In the future, the world was no longer dominated by humans. Humans, instead, survived in giant, aquatic airships. The airships were made of steel, and their hulls were ovoid and multiple stories tall, like a supersized submarine. The inside of each airship was filled with "water". I say "water" in quotes because it, functionally, acted no differently than air, as people could breathe in it, and and objects could pass through it as though it were air.
>
> One of these airships:
>
> - Sailed through an ocean of _Avatar_-style floating islands.
> - Contained a mess hall, where someone had just received their order.
> - Had Alis on board.

---

> In this world, Alis was the commander-in-chief of the remaining human forces.
>
> The way he conducted himself was fairly anomalous. He retained his memory manipulation abilities, so he could very easily hide himself. He could literally pass through a person's field of view without notice. If he made his presence known, he could hold a conversation and then walk away, leaving his conversational partners none the wiser.
>
> The airship Alis was always under threat. "Know your enemies!" a poster on the walls proclaimed. A Scylla-like creature festooned the poster, providing an example of the sea monsters that lay beyond the ship's hull.
>
> Later that day, the ship's crew encounters more sea monsters, and, with reluctance, launch torpedoes on that ship, those filled with "real" water. The crew's supply of torpedoes was growing limited, so they had to pick and choose their fights.

---

> As it turned out, Alis secretly regretted the world that had come to pass. Apparently, this reality was the product of a petty argument he had taken way, way, _way_ too far. He had immeasurably more clout than in the original world, to the point where he was effectively a god, yes, but he had consigned himself to a cripplingly lonely existence, as a result.
>
> ...Except, there was one other person.
>
> As it turns out, Solana had made it into this reality as well. In the previous reality, she was Alis's friend, but in this one, she was just one of the ship's passengers.
>
> Alis starts a conversation with Solana, who, in response, doesn't seem to recognize him. He asks what Solana thinks of their situation, and she, not knowing any better, doesn't see anything wrong.
>
> It turns out, though, that Alis couldn't fully manipulate Solana—at least, not 100%. "It would be nice," she admits to him, "if [we had something specific from the old world.]"
>
> Alis thanks Solana for her time.

---

> Suddenly, the sensors on the ship beep—the ship was entering the proximity of an enemy faction. The ship's crew prepares to send a communication line out to work out a peaceful passage through. The comms link is established, and communication with the leader of the enemy faction is reached. The hologram representing this person is depicted as an abstract 3D shape, vaguely resembling Alis if he were upside-down. To me, this seemed like deliberate symbolism.
>
> At this point, I, as the watcher, remarked how many creative liberties the animation studio took with my source material. Strangely enough, I only noticed after the developments in the previous paragraph. Regardless, I thought it was sick as hell seeing someone produce a movie-quality anime of my OCs.
>
> The movie, then, suggested something interesting: everything we watched may or may not have been hypothetical. We see Alis right before he would have made that "one petty decision" that caused the world to become post-apocalyptic. The movie suggests that everything up to this point had been a vision of the future, of which he was now aware. Now, Alis had to choose again.
>
> In retrospect, I did not like this plot development. The way the movie presented it made me feel like it was a cop-out, trying to backtrack out of the ending,
